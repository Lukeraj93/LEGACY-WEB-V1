How To Shoot Meal Photos

Keep the meal clear, well lit, and easy to identify before you upload it for analysis.

Top Down Center the whole plate

Hold the phone above the meal so the full plate, bowl, or tray is visible inside the frame.

Second Angle Use a 45 degree shot

Add a second angle when layers, sauces, toppings, or portion depth are hard to see from above.

Detail Include labels or packaging

Take a close-up of packaging, drinks, or sauces when the brand or serving size matters.

Best Practice

  • Use bright natural or overhead light.
  • Keep the phone steady and the meal in focus.
  • Show sauces, drinks, and sides separately when possible.

Avoid

  • Dark shadows, heavy filters, and motion blur.
  • Cropped plates where food is cut off.
  • Uploading only the packaging when the actual meal is plated.

How To Take Progress Photos

Keep the setup consistent every time so your coach can compare real changes, not different angles or lighting.

Front Stand tall and relaxed

Face the camera, feet hip-width apart, arms relaxed slightly away from your sides, full body in frame.

Side Turn 90 degrees

Stand in a natural side pose with your posture neutral and your body not twisted toward the camera.

Back Match the same framing

Keep the same distance, camera height, and lighting as the front shot so the comparison stays clean.

Detail Use only if needed

Add a detail shot only when you want your coach to notice a specific area, posture change, or physique detail.

Wear

  • Fitted training clothes you are comfortable repeating each check-in.
  • Shorts, leggings, fitted tee, tank, sports bra, or crop top that shows your actual shape.
  • The same or very similar outfit each time for cleaner comparisons.

Camera Rules

  • Place the camera around chest height and 2 to 3 meters away.
  • Use a plain wall, even light, and keep your full body inside the frame.
  • Avoid mirrors, tilted phones, filters, jackets, hoodies, hats, and shoes.
